The Structure
Four layers. Each one exists for a reason.
Swipe sideways to see the whole diagram →
1
Field App
Runs in a browser on whatever's in the inspector's hand — a laptop, a tablet. Every weld, torque reading, NDE result, and drawing markup is written to the device first. No signal in the field is not a problem — it's the assumed condition, not the exception.
2
Sync Layer
The moment there's a connection, changes push up automatically — no "upload" button, no batch job someone has to remember to run. Nothing on the device is the permanent record; the device is just where the truth starts. Offline edits are never silently lost — if two people change the same record the conflict is caught, a deleted row stays deleted, and an old copy on a device never overwrites the cloud.
3
Secure Database
A real relational database (PostgreSQL), not a shared spreadsheet or a folder of files. Every table has row-level security — the database itself checks who's asking before it answers, on every single query, not just at the login screen: 33 of 33 tables, 107 policies. A PASS/FAIL weld or a recorded NDE result cannot be deleted by crew, and deleted rows are archived, never just gone.
4
Role-Based Access
Four kinds of accounts, each with a genuinely different view of the same job. Velocity, the owner, runs the platform and is the only one who can add, switch off or remove a company. A client admin runs their company's jobs, invites their own crew and customers and approves ITPs — never more than their own scope. Crew fill in only the sections they are given, every upload traced to them. A customer sees only the sections left on VIEW for them and cannot change anything.

Inside The Platform
What's actually built, not a roadmap.
Every item below is running on live jobs today, grouped the way the folder is organised. One build for everyone on the platform — when a tool gets sharper, every company has it on their next load.
Field records
Weld Traceability
Every weld with size, schedule, material, process, WPS, welder stamps, NDE method and result — completion tracked in diameter-inches. Repairs, cut-outs and tracers keep their own rows; rejects stay visible until they are cleared.
Isometric Drawings, Live
Load an IFC PDF, a photo of a marked-up print, or sketch on a true isometric grid. Marks carry real data: the welder's stencil and date on the field weld, an NDE stamp that writes the weld log and NDE register, a legend that learns the job's own words.
Certificates Read For You
Drop a stack of mill certificates. Every page is read: one row per heat, the same heat on many components, sideways pages turned, unreadable pages brought up to be typed, skipped pages held until someone deals with them. The MTR index and package file themselves.
Bolt-Up & Pressure Testing
Flange-by-flange torque log with the 30% / 60% / final sequence. Hydro and pneumatic packs: the chart disc is read into the pack, gauge certificates are checked for expiry, and a test boundary drawn on the sheet fills the pack's weld scope.
Docs, Photos & Vendor Uploads
One cabinet for drawings, reports, photos and certificates. Every upload is traced to a person; a fabricator's or vendor's upload lands as needs-review until someone accepts it — or rejects it with a reason they are told about.
Daily Reports
What happened each day — people on site, work performed, issues, progress — with the NCRs raised that day counted in from the register, and every report kept in the history.
Control & approval
ITP Approvals In The App
A responsible person on every inspection point. Submitting freezes a numbered revision; the client's admin approves or asks for changes; every approved revision is filed as a signed PDF. Edits after approval bump the revision automatically.
Job Flow Board
Nine stages — drawings, materials, welding, NDE, test, coating, installation, pre-commissioning, turnover — read live from the folder. A failed weld or pack, a revised drawing or an ITP still waiting turns its stage yellow. Stages you don't use drop out.
Needs Attention
One list for every mismatch the folder finds on its own: a stencil with no qualification, bolt-up marks without a torque log, a drawing revised under its welds, a certificate page that was skipped. Each item holds the flow until someone deals with it — and records who did.
One NCR Register
One numbered register for the whole job. A number is never reused and nothing is deleted — a mistake becomes VOID. Critical and major audit findings raise their NCR themselves and close it when the finding closes. It prints with the package.
Audit & Safety Reports
Built around findings: severity, type of harm, work impact and status, with a photo or a marked-up clause attached. Numbered reports, a real PDF built on the device, sent to the job's people and kept in the history.
Physical Completion
Shop welding inches, field welding inches (live from the weld log when left blank), flange assembly percent and pressure-test packages passed — one plain overall number everyone on the job sees the same way.
Welding program
Welder Qualification & Continuity
One welder log per job. Continuity is worked out from the welds themselves — every dated weld by that stamp and process extends it — and a weld made after a lapse, before qualification or outside the process is flagged. A continuity report prints per welder.
WPS & PQR Library
WPSs and their supporting PQRs are entered once for the company and picked by every job. A WPS number used on the weld log but not on file is flagged before anyone has to find it in an audit.
Weld Quality Compliance
An ISO 3834 compliance status read live from the tabs that own each topic — welders, WPS, filler metal, MTRs, PWHT, NDE, pressure tests, ITP, calibration, NCRs, records — each marked OK, CHECK or PROBLEM, with a button straight to where it's fixed.
Company & people
Company Library
Approved ITPs, WPSs and welders belong to the company, not one job. The next job starts ready: the ITP carries over marked as carried, welder continuity follows across every job, and every change goes into the library's history.
Who Sees What
A client admin runs their own jobs and invites their own crew and customers. Crew fill in only the sections they're given; a customer sees only the sections left on VIEW and never changes anything. Invite links expire; notices go to the person they are meant for.
Printed In Your Brand
The turnover book prints under your company's logo and name: cover, index and every section page stamped with the job. Your own cover, your own section names, only the sections you use — nineteen when you want them all.
Backups, Exports & Sharing
A monthly backup of every company's records, exports on request, and a share page for chosen sections and dates whose document links expire after seven days.
Activity & Notices
A history that names the person behind every change, prints with the package, and notices for approvals, holds, shared packages and rejected uploads — so nobody has to ask what happened.
The Point
Every one of these tools reads and writes the same underlying record.
A weld status changed from the field shows up on the drawing, in the Job Flow board, in the completion number and in the log — all at once, because it's one system, not a dozen that happen to share a login page.
