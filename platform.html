The Platform
Built for speed. Built to make your team know the code, not just follow it.
This industry doesn't wait, and your paperwork shouldn't either. Velocity exists to make the field move faster — and to train the people doing the work until they understand the code instead of just checking a box. Defending it in front of a Board is just what happens once that's already true.
The Structure
Four layers. Each one exists for a reason.
Swipe sideways to see the whole diagram →
1
Field App
Runs in a browser on whatever's in the inspector's hand — a laptop, a tablet. Every weld, torque reading, NDE result, and drawing markup is written to the device first. No signal in the field is not a problem — it's the assumed condition, not the exception.
2
Sync Layer
The moment there's a connection, changes push up automatically — no "upload" button, no batch job someone has to remember to run. Nothing on the device is the permanent record; the device is just where the truth starts.
3
Secure Database
A real relational database (PostgreSQL), not a shared spreadsheet or a folder of files. Every table has row-level security — the database itself checks who's asking before it answers, on every single query, not just at the login screen.
4
Role-Based Access
Four kinds of accounts, each with a genuinely different view of the same job: our staff see everything; a client-side admin can invite their own inspectors and stand up new work orders once billing is active — never more than their own scope; a client sees their whole package, read-only; a sub sees only the sections they were explicitly granted.
Inside The Platform
What's actually built, not a roadmap.
Every item below is running on live jobs today. One build for everyone on the platform — when a tool gets sharper, every company has it on their next load.
Weld Traceability
Every weld logged with size, process, welder stamp, NDE method and result — completion tracked in diameter-inches, the unit the industry actually measures. The welder's stencil and date sit on the field-weld mark; an NDE stamp placed near a weld on the drawing writes the weld log and the NDE register itself. Repairs, cut-outs and tracers keep their own rows.
Isometric Drawings, Live
Load an IFC PDF, a photo of a marked-up print, or sketch on a true isometric grid. Markers carry real data — edit the mark and the log updates, edit the log and the drawing follows. A legend that learns the job's own words, pick-up callouts, multi-sheet, your own symbol library, sign-off built in.
Certificates Read For You
Drop a stack of mill certificates. Every page is read: one row per heat, duplicate pages set aside, sideways pages turned and retried, unreadable pages come up on screen to be typed. The MTR INDEX and the MTR PACKAGE file themselves as PDFs; rod and wire certificates go to Filler Metal.
Bolt-Up & Pressure Testing
Flange-by-flange torque log with the real 30%/60%/final sequence, wrench serials and acceptance dates. Hydro and pneumatic packs with pass/fail; the chart-recorder disc is read into the pack, gauge certificates are checked for expiry, and a test boundary drawn on the sheet fills the pack's weld scope.
ITP Approvals In The App
A responsible person on every inspection point. Submitting freezes a numbered revision; the client's admin approves or asks for changes on a banner; the approved copy prints with its stamp. Edits after approval bump the revision automatically — no emailed PDFs going back and forth.
Job Flow Board
Nine stages — drawings, materials, welding, NDE, test, coating, installation, pre-commissioning, turnover — read live from the folder. A failed weld or pack, a drawing revised under its welds, or an ITP still waiting turns its stage yellow. Percent per stage and progress over time, on one board.
One Completion Number
Welding, bolt-up, and pressure testing each contribute a weighted share to one overall physical-completion percentage — the number a project manager actually wants, computed live instead of assembled by hand before a meeting.
Quality Audit Per Item
Eleven audit items rated on their own — welding, NDE, materials, WPS/WPQ, PWHT, pressure test, ISO 3834, NCR, equipment, closeout. The verdict follows the ratings. Numbered reports, a real PDF built on the device, sent to the job's people and filed in Docs & Photos, every report kept in the history.
Needs Attention
One list for every mismatch the folder finds on its own: a welder stencil with no qualification on file, bolt-up marks without a torque log, a drawing revised under its welds, a certificate page that was skipped. Each item holds the flow until someone deals with it — and records who did.
Printed In Your Brand
The data book prints under your company's logo and name, not ours: cover, index and every section page stamped with the job, a confidential line at the foot of every sheet. Your own cover, your own section names, only the sections you use — nineteen when you want them all.
Invite Your Own Team
Once your account is active, add your own inspectors and stand up new work orders yourself. Invite links expire; every upload is traced to a person; notices and an activity history say who did what and when. Company exports and a monthly backup are yours to download.
The Point
Every one of these tools reads and writes the same underlying record.
A weld status changed from the field shows up on the drawing, in the Job Flow board, in the completion scorecard, and in the log — all at once, because it's one system, not a dozen that happen to share a login page.
